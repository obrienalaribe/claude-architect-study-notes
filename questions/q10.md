# Question 10

Domain 5, Context Management and Reliability. Task statement 5.5.

## Scenario

A hospital group's procurement team extracts fields from four kinds of supplier document. Overall accuracy on a labelled set is 94.8%, and the operations lead wants to stop human review of every extraction the model marks high confidence. Two reviewers cover all four document types.

## Question

What should happen before review is reduced?

- **A.** Go ahead, since the overall figure shows that high-confidence results can be trusted across every document type.
- **B.** Break accuracy down by document type and by field, and keep sampling high-confidence results after the change.
- **C.** Raise the high-confidence cut-off to 0.99, automate everything above it, and send the rest to the two reviewers.
- **D.** Keep full human review of every extraction for good, whatever the accuracy data shows.

<details>
<summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

**Answer: B.** One overall figure can hide a document type or field that performs badly. Checking each segment first, then sampling high-confidence output on an ongoing basis, shows where automation is safe and catches new error patterns.

Why the others fail:

- **A.** An aggregate number can mask a weak document type or field.
- **C.** A stricter cut-off is still uncalibrated and still unchecked per segment.
- **D.** It spends two reviewers on everything instead of directing them to low-confidence and ambiguous cases.

</details>
