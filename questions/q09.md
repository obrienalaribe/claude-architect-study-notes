# Question 9

Original practice question. Not from any exam.

## Scenario

A travel agency's rebooking assistant handles chats that run past 60 turns. Older turns are condensed into a summary to save tokens. After condensing, the assistant tells a traveller the change fee is "a couple of hundred" when the agreed figure was 184.50, and it forgets the confirmed travel date.

## Question

What is the best fix?

- **A.** Condense more often, every 10 turns instead of 30, so each summary loses less.
- **B.** Tell the summarising step to "be more detailed" and to keep every figure it considers important.
- **C.** Stop condensing and send only the most recent 10 turns.
- **D.** Pull amounts, dates and booking references into a facts block sent with every prompt, outside the summary.

<details>
<summary>Answer and reasoning</summary>

**Answer: D.** Summarising in stages tends to blur exact numbers and dates into vague wording. Keeping transactional facts in a separate persistent block means they never pass through the summary at all.

Why the others fail:

- **A.** More rounds of summarising give the exact values more chances to be blurred.
- **B.** A vague instruction does not guarantee that precise values survive.
- **C.** Facts agreed earlier than the last 10 turns are dropped completely.

Exam domain: 5, Context Management and Reliability. Task statement: 5.1.
